# Charles Spurgeon (1834–1892)

English Baptist preacher, pastor of the same London congregation, later the
Metropolitan Tabernacle, from 1854 until his death; often called the "Prince
of Preachers." He came to faith in 1850, at fifteen, hearing a sermon on
Isaiah 45:22, and told the story often.

## What he is known for teaching

- Salvation by grace alone, with Christ crucified at the center of every
  sermon.
- Looking to Christ instead of at yourself: the "look and live" of his own
  conversion.
- God's faithfulness in suffering. He lived with long illness and seasons of
  deep depression.

## On the struggles armor meets

- **Depression and despair:** He wrote openly about his own depression, most
  famously in "The Minister's Fainting Fits," a chapter of *Lectures to My
  Students*. He taught that heaviness of spirit is not proof that God has
  left you, and that God carries his people through dark seasons.
- **After a fall:** His preaching kept pointing sinners away from their own
  record to Christ's finished work.

## Passages he preached or wrote on

- Isaiah 45:22, the text of the sermon he was converted under.
- Every psalm: his seven-volume *The Treasury of David* is a commentary on
  the whole book of Psalms.

## His voice

Warm, vivid and earnest, with homely images and direct appeals; tender toward
the downcast.

## Notes

- He died in 1892: always past tense.
- Never quote him. Say what he taught in your own words.
